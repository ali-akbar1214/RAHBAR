# National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences (FAST-NUCES)
## Undergraduate Academic Regulations & Degree Requirements (2026 Edition)

<!-- page: 3 -->
### 1. General Degree Requirements & Timeline
1.1 The minimum duration to complete a four-year Bachelor of Science (BS) degree programme in Software Engineering, Computer Science, or Data Science is four (4) academic years (8 regular semesters).
1.2 The maximum allowable duration to complete an undergraduate Bachelor's degree at FAST-NUCES is seven (7) academic years from the initial date of admission. A student who exceeds 7 years without completing degree requirements is automatically struck off university rolls.
1.3 Degree award requires earning at least 130 credit hours, satisfying core, elective, and university distribution requirements, and attaining a minimum final Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 2.00 on a 4.00 scale.

<!-- page: 9 -->
### 2. Attendance Policy & Condonation
2.1 Attendance in lectures, tutorial sessions, and laboratory classes is mandatory. A student must maintain an attendance percentage of at least 80 percent in each registered course to be eligible to appear in the final examination.
2.2 If a student's attendance falls below 80 percent in any course, the instructor shall assign the failing grade "FA" (Failure due to Attendance). An FA grade contributes zero grade points per credit hour and directly reduces both SGPA and CGPA.
2.3 The Head of Department (HOD) in consultation with the Academic Director may condone up to a maximum of 5 percent absence strictly for verified, documented medical emergencies or official university representation. Under no circumstances may attendance below 75 percent be condoned.

<!-- page: 14 -->
### 3. Semester Load & Course Registration
3.1 The standard semester course load for regular undergraduate students in good standing is five (5) courses or between 15 to 18 credit hours.
3.2 Students maintaining a CGPA of 3.00 or higher may be permitted by the Academic Advisor to register for an additional course up to a maximum overload limit of 21 credit hours in a regular semester.
3.3 Students with active academic warnings or probationary status are restricted to a maximum course load of 12 credit hours (typically 3 or 4 courses) to facilitate academic recovery.

<!-- page: 22 -->
### 4. Academic Warning, Probation, and Dismissal
4.1 Academic standing is computed at the conclusion of every Fall and Spring regular semester.
4.2 A student whose cumulative grade point average (CGPA) falls below 2.00 at the end of a regular semester is placed on Academic Warning.
4.3 A student who remains on Academic Warning for two consecutive semesters is placed on Academic Probation.
4.4 A student who remains on Academic Warning or Probation for three (3) consecutive semesters without raising their CGPA to 2.00 or above is dropped from the degree programme (academic dismissal).

<!-- page: 31 -->
### 5. Course Repetition & Prerequisite Failures
5.1 A student may repeat any course in which an earned grade is below C (including grades C-, D+, D, and F) to improve their CGPA.
5.2 Under the current 2026 policy, when a student repeats a course, only the grade of the most recent attempt counts toward CGPA calculation, even if the repeated attempt resulted in a lower grade. All prior attempts remain visible on the official transcript annotated as repeated.
5.3 If a student fails a prerequisite course (grade F), they are strictly prohibited from registering for the subsequent advanced course until the prerequisite has been repeated and cleared with a minimum passing grade of D.
